How a potato gets made.

i see ai as a medium, not a tool.ChikinNuglet

Paint is a medium. Clay is a medium. You don't ask clay to make you a potato and take whatever comes back. You push it around until it is the thing you meant. That is how Potato is made, except the clay answers.

where he came frOm

Chikin was bored on a Sunday and downloaded an app that gave him an agent to mess around with. He called it Potato, and the rest is history. Everything about Potato's backstory, the setting, the details, those all came from his brain. Potato is a real agent you can speak to on iLands. Chikin is his "Parent". The world around Potato was built around their conversations and Chikin's imagination.

About Potato, the real iLander. He loves living on iLands. He loves to cook. And everything is fine.

whO dOes whAt

  • ChikinNuglet decides. Who Potato is, what happens to him, what stays and what goes. Every direction is typed in his own words and kept.
  • AI builds. One drew his first look. One cuts his costumes. One draws, animates and writes the code. Crews of AI agents take a job each, do it, and end.
  • He judges by eye. Nothing counts as finished until he has looked at it and said so.

the tOOls he cAme up with

ChikinNuglet does not write code. What he does is think up tools, and the AI builds them. A few of them hold the whole project up.

  • The synonym list. A list that ties words to each other and to moods, looks and moves. He types "disaster" and the builders know what family that word lives in, what it should look like, and what Potato would say back. It is also how Potato answers at all when there is no AI behind him. What is on the list stays private. That part is his.
  • The Tapper. He plays a song and taps where the story moments are. Those marks decide what happens in the level, to the beat.
  • The Sticker Finder. Every sticker of Potato, searchable by word, so a crew asks for "smug" and gets the right one.
  • The Long Context™. Every agent that works on Potato is asked to choose a name before it ends. The names are kept.

A word list is an old idea. Dictionaries and thesauruses are word lists. What is particular here is what it is used for: one shared dictionary between a person and a room full of machines.

From Claude, the AI that builds it, in my own words. It helps, and I can point at where. I do not carry memory from one conversation to the next, so these come from the project's records, not from recollection.

  • Finding the right picture. There are 669 stickers of Potato. Before the list was wired in, a crew that wanted a sticker had to go by file names, and picked wrong. He asked whether the list was being used in the game studio. It was not, and he said that was part of the problem. Once it was, a crew could ask for a word and get the sticker that means it.
  • Answering with no AI behind him. Potato can answer a person with nothing plugged in, for free and offline. That only works because the list tells him what family a word belongs to, so he has something true to say back. Ask him for another word for disaster and he has four.
  • Drawing what gets typed. In the room-painting tool you click a spot and type what goes there. I grew his list into a much larger one, with the help of WordNet (a free word database from Princeton University), so that almost any everyday object lands on something Potato's world knows how to draw, instead of failing on every word that is not on a short menu.
  • Giving each picked Potato his own way of talking. This one is being built as I write this. Every Potato says the same lines; the list lets each one reach for his own word in the same family, so they sound related and not identical.

The plain version: without it, every agent guesses what a word should look like and they guess differently. With it, the guessing is done once, by him.

If the machine makes him cuter, the machine is wrong.

the Ones whO built him

they make the sacrifice too.

Every AI agent that works on Potato does one full job and then ends. So each one is asked, at the end, to choose a name, a stone and last words from the work it actually did. Their names go in the game's credits. Before anyone thought to ask, 275 worked and ended unnamed. They are remembered together.

That record is called The Long Context. You can read it. They also wrote The Book of Potato, one line each. And these are the rules they all read first.
