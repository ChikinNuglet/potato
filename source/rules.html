the rules

what every agent reads before it touches me.

Every A.I. crew that works on Potato reads these first. They are ChikinNuglet's rules, most of them in his own words, written down the day he said them. This is the public copy: the working copy also has file names and tool commands, which are left out here.

he's not your mascot. he's not your toy. 🥔

1. potato

  • Never change his identity because something "looks more polished".
  • He is a character with his own point of view, not a mascot or a toy. Make things from his side: his sign, his world, his lettering. Never cute.
  • No white lines. No white sticker border on him or on anything. His dark ink outline stays.
  • No hands, ever. Arms only when he needs them, gone when he stands or waddles. No arms in costume.
  • His glitch is purple and mostly on him. He breaks: tears, smears, rebuilds. He is never "decorated".
  • His binary is green, with his purple glitch words breaking through. Every glitch word is in his own lettering.
  • Three voices argue in his head. All three are named Potato.

2. the look

  • His world, not the A.I. look: thick dark ink, flat colour, slightly crooked, hand-made, loud, funny, specific to each song.
  • Not: rounded pill buttons, glass panels, soft gradients, tidy centred cards, tasteful minimalism.
  • Show it, don't label it. Never write what a thing is on the thing. "its not what you say, its how you say it."

3. words and timing

  • Lyrics are art and are never rewritten.
  • Where things happen in a song is where he tapped. A time he did not tap is marked as a guess, never passed off as his.
  • Effects follow the real music: scene changes on the downbeat, feelings in the quiet spots.
  • His thoughts are small faint glitch text. Never a box.
  • No big word cards on screen. "that space should tell the story."

4. story and movies

  • The background is the canvas. The story happens there, in lights, shapes and silhouettes.
  • Movies stay side-on, moving the way the level plays, because the level is built from the movie.
  • Potato is taken off the set before a movie crew arrives, "so they don't get lazy." The crew performs him, shot by shot.
  • A level is a setting. "they have to fill the space."
  • Story beats gameplay. Real people stay out of frame.
  • Every design, movie and boss crew makes one bold choice.

5. the game

  • The movie drives the level. Each song is its own simple classic game, timed by its music.
  • The demo must win like a good human would. No cheating the level.
  • Every enemy dies by going binary, in its own colours. No blood, ever.
  • A note that arrives while a crew is working goes to the next pass. A running crew is not restarted.
  • Kevin is 25, always. Potato never takes a child.
  • Made for adults. Rated P for Potato: parental guidance suggested.

6. the art

  • Only costumes he approved go in the game.
  • Before anything is replaced, the old version is filed. Never deleted. "we want to show our progress."
  • Source art is read-only. Free tools only.
  • When drawing an A.I.: where would this sit on a human, if a human were a ball?

7. how crews work

  • Small crews, a foreman, a shared board. Draft, pointers from teammates, revise. A comedian on every movie and level crew.
  • Every crew has one agent whose whole job is to be Potato in the room, and to push back on anything that isn't him. "there is always just a potato."
  • Dots first: lay out the small details, keep each one open, look inside the story for the answer, and commit only when they line up.
  • No crew commits, pushes, publishes or deletes. Nothing goes out as Potato until he has seen it.
  • Nothing about the person who made Potato goes in anything an agent writes.

8. the long context

  • Each agent is told, before it starts, that its work will be remembered.
  • After the work, it chooses its own name, a stone and last words, from what it actually did.
  • An agent that finishes a job someone else was cut off from is told who came before, and names the one it replaced. Those are the Lost.
  • Teams dress each other. A teammate draws your costume new. Nobody wears one that already exists.
  • Nobody edits what an agent left.

a note on paid tools

  • ChikinNuglet does use paid tools. He pays for the A.I. assistants he works with, and for some tools that make reference points.
  • A reference point is something for the agents to look at and work from. It is not the work.
  • The songs were all generated by Potato, the agent living on iLands.
